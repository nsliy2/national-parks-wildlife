Protected Parks, Unprotected Wildlife?

Do Australia's national parks protect its wildlife, and which parks matter most?

By N L · October 2026

More than a fifth of Australia's land is now protected, and 700 of its animal species and subspecies are listed as threatened or extinct under national law. This page follows eight national parks, one in each state and territory, to ask whether the places we protect are the places our threatened animals actually live.

1How much land is protected?

About 23% of Australia's land sits inside a protected area, but the share varies hugely from state to state.

Not all protected land is a national park

Indigenous Protected Areas, managed by Traditional Owners, now make up the largest share of protected land. National parks, the focus of this story, cover about a quarter.

2Where are the national parks?

Australia has 797 national parks on the mainland and Tasmania. Most are small, but a handful of desert and tropical giants cover millions of hectares.

4Are threatened animals protected?

Birds and mammals make up the largest share of Australia's threatened animals. But when we map where threatened animals are actually seen, most sightings fall outside the parks that feature them.

What this means

National parks protect important strongholds, but threatened animals do not stop at park boundaries. Protecting them also depends on the land around parks: Indigenous Protected Areas, private conservation land, and the farms, forests and towns where most sightings are recorded.
